## Design Context

### Users
Dual audience: crypto-native Stacks users who understand wallets and on-chain mechanics, and casual players drawn in by the simplicity of the game loop. Both groups share one thing — they want the experience to feel premium and worth their time/money.

### Brand Personality
**Premium / Addictive / Fast**

Three words as physical objects: a heavy casino chip sliding across a felt table, a card dealt at speed, a payout that lands before you expected it.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark — users play this in the evening, in their browser, with real money on the line. The dark surface makes the gold pop and keeps focus on the game.
- **Fonts**: Cinzel (display/headings) — Roman-inscribed caps, premium card-game gravitas. Barlow (body) — geometric humanist, clean, slightly condensed, "fast".
- **Colors**: Keep existing dark surfaces (#0f0f14 base, #16161e raised, #1e1e2a overlay) and gold (#d4a84b). Extend with win/loss semantic colors (emerald green, rose red). All in OKLCH.
- **References**: High-end online casino, creative betting platform. Not garish Vegas — refined Monaco.
- **Anti-references**: Cheap crypto gambling sites, neon-everything, glassmorphism glow everywhere.

### Design Principles
1. **Gold earns its place** — The accent color signals value. Use it on numbers that matter (balances, payouts, wins) and on CTAs. Never decoratively.
2. **Speed is felt** — Interactions snap. Animations are short (150–250ms), easing is exponential out. No lazy fades.
3. **The game is the hero** — Every other page supports the play loop. Navigation is secondary. The play button is always reachable.
4. **Empty states teach** — When there's no history, no wins, no balance — the UI explains what the user needs to do next, not just that nothing's there.
5. **Hierarchy over decoration** — Size, weight, and spacing do the work. Not borders, badges, or gradients.
